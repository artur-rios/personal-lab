The five principles of object-oriented design that Robert C. Martin set out and
Michael Feathers named SOLID: single responsibility, open-closed, Liskov
substitution, interface segregation and dependency inversion. Each article
explains one principle, where it came from, what it looks like in running code
and when it is worth applying, and the series closes with inversion of control,
the mechanism that puts the last of them to work.
